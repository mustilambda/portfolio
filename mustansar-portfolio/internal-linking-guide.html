The AI-Powered Internal
Linking Prompt

Internal links are one of the highest-ROI SEO moves you can make — and most writers ignore them. This guide explains why they matter and gives you a copy-paste prompt to find 20+ linking opportunities for any article in minutes.

SEO ⏱ 6 min read 4 steps 1 prompt

Why Internal Links Are a Silent Ranking Signal

When Google crawls your website, it follows links. Every internal link you place is a direct instruction to the crawler — go here next, this page matters, these two topics are connected. Without them, your best content sits in isolation, undiscovered by both search engines and readers.

Internal links do three things simultaneously: they distribute PageRank (link equity) across your site, they signal topical authority by connecting related content, and they improve user experience by guiding readers to what they need next — which reduces bounce rate and increases time on site, both of which Google watches closely.

40% of pages on average websites have zero internal links pointing to them
3× more PageRank flows to pages with strong internal linking vs orphan pages
20+ linking opportunities this prompt surfaces for any article on any site

What internal links actually do for your rankings

  • Crawlability: Google can only index what it can find. Internal links create clear pathways for crawlers to reach every important page.
  • Topical clusters: When related articles link to each other, Google understands your site covers a topic in depth — not just in passing.
  • Anchor text signals: The clickable text of your link tells Google what the destination page is about. Keyword-aligned anchor text is a ranking signal.
  • PageRank flow: High-authority pages (like your homepage) can pass ranking power to newer or weaker pages through internal links.
  • Reduced orphan pages: Pages with no inbound internal links are invisible to crawlers. Every link you add rescues a page from obscurity.

The problem most teams have: Internal linking is tedious to do manually. You'd need to read every existing article, judge its relevance, craft anchor text, and decide where to place the link. For a site with 50+ posts, that's hours of work per article. This prompt does it in seconds.

Two directions that most people miss

Most writers only think about linking out from their new article. But the bigger win is inbound links — updating your existing posts to link back to the new article you just published. A new page with zero inbound internal links starts life as an orphan. Give it links from day one and it ranks faster.

This prompt handles both directions: which old posts should link to your new article, and which existing posts your new article should link out to.

The Prompt

Copy the prompt below exactly. You'll fill in three inputs before you paste it into your AI tool.

📋 Internal Linking Prompt — Copy & Paste 
You are an expert SEO strategist specialising in internal linking.

Before you begin, I will provide you with three inputs:

1. WEBSITE URL: [Paste your website URL here — e.g. https://www.example.com]

2. TARGET KEYWORDS: [Paste your primary keyword and any secondary keywords here — e.g. "best gaming laptops, gaming laptop under $1000, gaming laptop 2025"]

3. ARTICLE CONTENT: [Paste your full article or a detailed summary here]

---

Now proceed with the following:

Using Google Search with site:[Website URL], find existing posts on this site that are topically relevant to the article and keywords above. Your goal is to identify at least 20 highly relevant internal linking opportunities, split into two directions.

---

PART A — Inbound Links (Existing posts that should link TO the new post)

For each candidate, provide:
- Post Title & URL
- Relevance Score (1–10)
- Why it should link here — shared subtopic, keyword overlap, or reader journey logic
- Suggested anchor text — 3–5 words, natural and keyword-aligned
- Placement — intro, body, or conclusion

Rank by relevance score, highest first. Find 10–12 candidates.

---

PART B — Outbound Links (The new post linking OUT to existing posts)

For each candidate, provide:
- Post Title & URL
- Relevance Score (1–10)
- Why the new post should link here — what value does this add for the reader?
- Suggested anchor text — 3–5 words, natural and keyword-aligned
- Contextual fit — which section or topic within the new post this link belongs in

Rank by relevance score, highest first. Find 8–10 candidates.

---

PART C — Priority Summary

- Top 3 inbound links to implement first and why
- Top 3 outbound links to add to the new post and why
- Any content gaps — related topics the site is missing that could strengthen the topical cluster

---

Present all results in a clean table format. Every recommendation must serve both the reader experience and search intent. Do not suggest tangential or loosely related pages.

How to Use This in 4 Steps

The whole workflow takes under 5 minutes. Here's exactly what to do:

1
Gather your three inputs Copy your website URL, list your target keywords (primary + secondary), and have your article ready to paste — either the full text or a detailed summary.
2
Fill in the prompt Paste the prompt above into a text editor. Replace the three placeholder sections with your URL, keywords, and article content. Keep everything else exactly as written.
3
Run it in Grok with Deep Research Open Grok, paste your filled prompt, select Deep Research mode, and hit submit. Deep Research lets it actually crawl your site and return verified links.
4
Add the links to your article Use the output tables to update your new post (outbound links) and go back into your existing posts to add the inbound links it identified. Start with Part C's top 3 priorities.

Pro tip: Don't skip the inbound links in Part A. Updating 3–5 existing posts to link to your new article is the fastest way to accelerate its indexing and initial ranking. Google sees those links within days of your edits being crawled.

Which AI Tool to Use

This prompt works with any AI that has live web search access. For best results, use one of these three — each handles the site: search operator reliably.

Grok (Deep Research) ★ Best
Gemini (Deep Research) ★ Best
Claude (with Search) ★ Best
  • Grok with Deep Research — the recommended default. Deep Research mode actively browses your site and validates that every URL it returns actually exists.
  • Gemini with Deep Research — equally strong. Google's own search infrastructure means excellent site: operator support.
  • Claude with web search enabled — good for topical analysis and anchor text suggestions; pair with search turned on for best link accuracy.

Always verify URLs before publishing. AI tools occasionally hallucinate links. Paste each suggested URL into your browser to confirm the page exists before adding the link to your article.

What the Output Looks Like

The AI will return three structured tables. Here's what to expect from each:

Part A — Inbound link table

A ranked list of your existing posts that should now link to your new article, with a suggested anchor text phrase and where in that post the link should go (intro, a specific section, or conclusion). You'll take this list and go update those old posts.

Part B — Outbound link table

A ranked list of existing posts your new article should link out to, with anchor text and the specific section within your new article where each link fits most naturally.

Part C — Priority summary + content gaps

The 3 most important links to implement in each direction, plus a list of topics your site is missing that would strengthen your topical cluster. Use the gaps list to plan your next articles — covering those topics builds the kind of authority that moves rankings at scale.
