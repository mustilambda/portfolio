How to Write Any Commercial Page

Writing great commercial pages isn't about creativity anymore. It's about research, psychology, and structure. Spend 60–80% of your time researching before you write a single word.

⏱ 10 min read 📋 5 sections ✅ Checklist included

The rule of thumb: The more manual the work, the better the result. There are no shortcuts here — only a proven process that separates pages that convert from pages that just exist.

Phase A

Research — 60 to 80% of Your Time

Before writing a word, you need to understand the product, the brand, and the people buying it. Open the page and study the service or product first. Know the brand USPs, product USPs, target audience, key features, and the specific goal of the page.

Know Your Audience in 3 Layers

Loyal Customers

Already know your product is the best. Reinforce why they made the right choice.

New Customers

Hit their tipping point — the specific moment or frustration that triggered their search.

Almost-Buyers

On the fence. Address their objections directly and remove friction to commit.

Your copy should speak to all three unless the brief specifies otherwise.

⚡ Advanced: VOC Mining (The Secret Weapon)

Mine Reddit, Amazon reviews, and forums for the exact language your customers use — not the language marketers think they use. Interview customers if possible and ask:

  1. What triggered you to search for this?
  2. What almost stopped you from buying?
  3. What does life look like before vs. after using this?

Organise findings into four buckets: pain points, goals, objections, and desired outcomes. This language goes directly into your copy.

Competitor Analysis

Search the target keyword on Google. Review the headings of the top 10 results and note the industry-specific terms and entities competitors use. Use keyword clustering — grouping related terms — instead of repeating one keyword. Find direct quotes or sticky phrases. Keep an eye on competitor page design for CRO ideas.

Building the Outline

  • Create a rough outline manually, then use AI to refine it
  • Extract headings from your research and merge them into a final structure
  • Map the user journey — think about where each heading sits in the page flow
  • You can use AI to generate an outline via competitor analysis prompting, but always verify it manually
  • (Optional) Use tools like v0 or Claude to generate a visual page design with your branding and CRO guidelines
Phase B

Writing — 30% of Your Time

If you can't write the crux of the page in the headline, don't write. The headline either carries the whole page or kills it.

Writing Principles

  • Benefits over features — always answer "so what?" after any feature claim
  • Active voice — avoid passive constructions that dilute impact
  • No "whether" or "while" sentence structures — they weaken copy
  • No AI clichés: eliminate "transform," "leverage," "imagine," "picture this," and em dashes
  • No jargon — if it needs explaining, it needs removing
  • If a line can be removed without affecting quality, remove it

Make Content "Liftable"

  • Write in 1–3 sentence standalone blocks — each section should make sense on its own
  • Aim for 120–180 words per section as optimal length
  • Use bullets and tables — AI and humans both love structured data
  • Less content, more visualisation wherever possible
  • Include reviews and testimonials as trust anchors — non-negotiable
Phases C · D · E

CTA Rules, Location Pages & FAQs

✦ CTA Rules

No generic CTAs. Use multiple CTAs with the same intent — different wording each time. Add urgency and promotions where possible. One CTA immediately after the hero section, one after the last section — non-negotiable. Include phone numbers if the service is call-driven.

✦ Location Pages

H1 must include the location name. Embed a Google Map where possible. Mention local landmarks to signal relevance. Use NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistently. Tailor the service list to local context.

✦ FAQ Rules

Pull FAQs from the SERP "People Also Ask" box — most credible source. Match FAQ type to page intent: MOFU + BOFU for collection pages, BOFU only for product and home pages.

✦ FAQ Writing

Keep answers under 30 words. Write and answer in a direct tone — no ambiguity. You can use bullets inside answers. Never hedge or qualify — be certain and specific.

✅ Commercial Page Writing Checklist

Pre-Writing Research

Writing Phase

Final Check

Headline compelling, first paragraph hooks immediately Benefits answer "So what?" for every claim All CTAs are action-focused with urgency Mobile-friendly layout confirmed Trust signals present throughout Proofread and final polish done
